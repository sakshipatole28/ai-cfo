AI CFO

A short introduction for a chartered accountant firm

Every client's investments in one place, checked, explained and ready for review.

AI CFO collects a client's shares and mutual funds from their statements and, with their permission, directly from their broker. It works out the exact numbers, and then drafts plain-language notes with the rule behind each one. A professional approves everything before the client sees it.

What it does

Four things, done carefully.

1. Shows what the client really owns

It opens up every mutual fund to see the actual companies inside, so you can see, for example, that 14% of the money is in one bank.

2. Checks the plan

It compares the client's split between shares, bonds, gold and cash with the agreed target and shows the gap in rupees.

3. Tracks tax timing

It tracks each purchase date, flags purchases about to become long-term (taxed at 12.5% instead of 20%), and shows the unused tax-free allowance.

4. Explains, with sources

It drafts short notes in plain English that quote the rule they rely on, such as the Income-tax Act 2025 or SEBI regulations. It never gives buy or sell tips.

How it works

From scattered statements to an approved review.

The firm adds a client You

The client's name and PAN (Permanent Account Number) are locked inside your own browser. Our servers only see a code such as C-7F3A.

The client gives permission Client

The client reads what will be shared and why, and agrees with a one-time code on their phone. They can withdraw at any time.

Holdings come in, from statements and live from the broker Data

The monthly Consolidated Account Statement (one PDF that lists all mutual funds or all demat shares) covers everything. The live connection to the client's broker (below) shows today's shares. The two are compared, and any difference is flagged.

Exact calculations Tested code, no AI

Every number, including allocation, gaps from target, fund overlap, company exposure and tax on each purchase, is calculated by tested code and shows its formula and inputs.

AI agents decide what matters and explain it AI

Three "analyst" programs look at overlap, allocation and tax side by side. A writer drafts each finding and quotes the rule. A checker rejects advice-like wording and any number the calculations did not produce.

A professional approves You

You accept, edit or dismiss each finding, add your reasoning, and approve. Every action is recorded in a tamper-evident log.

Main feature · live connection to the client's broker

Today's holdings, not last month's statement.

The client connects their Dhan account once. This uses Dhan's official programming interface: the standard way one program asks another for data, with the account holder's approval.

1. Client signs in on DhanOn Dhan's own website, with their own password and phone code. AI CFO never sees either.
2. Client approves "view only"Dhan shows exactly what is shared: holdings and positions. Nothing else.
3. A 24-hour passDhan gives a temporary pass. AI CFO uses it once to read the holdings and then throws it away.
4. Compared and flaggedLive quantities are matched with the latest statement. Differences go to you, never merged silently.

Read-only by design: the software contains no instruction that can buy, sell or move money, and an automatic check blocks anyone from adding one.

Keeping data safe

Built so that even we cannot see who your clients are.

Names stay with you

Client names and PAN are encrypted in your browser with a key only your firm holds.

Kept in India

Real client data is stored and processed only in Indian data centres.

It follows the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: clear consent, use only for the stated purpose, and erasure on request.

Who looked at what, and when, is kept in a log that shows any tampering.

How to share data without identities

You remove identities on your own computer, before anything is sent.

A small tool runs on your computer (or in your browser, without uploading). It removes identity columns and replaces each client and folio (fund account) number with a code made from a secret key that only your firm keeps. The same client always gets the same code, but the code cannot be turned back into a name without your key.

Words used on this page

Glossary

Consolidated Account Statement
One PDF listing everything a person holds: mutual funds (from CAMS or KFintech) or demat shares (from NSDL or CDSL).
Demat account
An electronic account that holds shares, opened through a broker.
Broker
The company and app used to buy and sell shares, such as Dhan.
ISIN
International Securities Identification Number: the 12-character ID of every share and fund.
Folio
A client's account number with a mutual fund house.
Long-term and short-term gains
Profit on listed shares or equity funds held more than 12 months is long-term (12.5%, with ₹1.25 lakh a year tax-free); 12 months or less is short-term (20%).
Fund overlap
How much two funds hold the same companies. High overlap means paying for two funds that behave like one.
Programming interface (API)
The official way one program asks another for data, here with the account holder's approval.
AI agent
A program that uses an AI model to decide or write something, within limits we set and check.
SEBI
Securities and Exchange Board of India, the market regulator.
