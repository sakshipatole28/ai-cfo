## Consent and notice
Personal data may be processed on the basis of the person's consent, which must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action, and limited to the data needed for the stated purpose. The request for consent must come with a notice that describes the data, the purpose and how to withdraw consent or complain.

## Withdrawing consent
A person can withdraw consent at any time, and withdrawing must be as easy as giving consent. After withdrawal, the data fiduciary must stop processing and have its processors stop, unless the law requires the data to be kept.

## Data fiduciary and data processor
The data fiduciary decides why and how personal data is processed and remains responsible for compliance, including for processing carried out by a data processor on its behalf under a contract. In a software-as-a-service setting, the advisory firm is usually the data fiduciary and the software provider is the data processor.

## Security, breaches and erasure
Data fiduciaries must take reasonable security safeguards. A personal data breach must be reported to the Data Protection Board and to affected persons; the Rules require a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach. Personal data must be erased once the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless retention is required by law.

## Timeline and penalties
The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Most obligations on data fiduciaries, including notice, consent, security and breach reporting, apply 18 months after notification, from 13 May 2027. Penalties for some failures can reach ₹250 crore.
