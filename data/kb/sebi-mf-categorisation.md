## Market capitalisation buckets
Large cap companies are the 1st to 100th companies by full market capitalisation, mid cap companies are the 101st to 250th, and small cap companies are the 251st onwards. AMFI publishes the list of large, mid and small cap companies twice a year so that all fund houses use the same classification.

## Equity scheme categories
A large cap fund invests at least 80% of its assets in large cap stocks. A mid cap fund invests at least 65% in mid cap stocks and a small cap fund at least 65% in small cap stocks. A large and mid cap fund invests at least 35% each in large cap and mid cap stocks. A flexi cap fund invests at least 65% in equity with no restriction on market capitalisation.

## ELSS and index funds
An equity-linked savings scheme invests at least 80% in equity and has a three-year lock-in. Index funds and exchange-traded funds invest at least 95% of their assets in the securities of the index they track. Because large cap funds and Nifty 50 index funds draw from the same top 100 companies, holding both often produces high portfolio overlap.

## Debt scheme categories
A liquid fund invests in debt and money market instruments with maturity of up to 91 days. A short duration fund keeps the Macaulay duration of its portfolio between one and three years. Scheme names and categories must match these definitions so investors can compare like with like.
