## Default tax regime
The new tax regime is the default for individuals. It has lower slab rates but removes most deductions and exemptions. An individual can choose the old regime instead, which keeps deductions such as those for specified savings and insurance premiums. Advisers should confirm which regime a client uses before suggesting tax-saving investments.

## Section 123 deduction (formerly section 80C)
Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the deduction formerly known as section 80C is section 123, with the qualifying investments listed in a schedule. The overall limit remains ₹1.5 lakh a year. Qualifying items include contributions to the Public Provident Fund and Employees' Provident Fund, life insurance premiums, principal repayment of a home loan, and investments in equity-linked savings schemes (ELSS). The deduction is available only under the old regime.

## ELSS lock-in
Equity-linked savings schemes carry a lock-in of three years from the date of each investment. With a SIP, every instalment is locked for three years from its own date, so units become free for redemption in a rolling pattern. Gains on ELSS units are taxed like other equity-oriented funds when sold.

## Rebate and special-rate income
Under the new regime, the rebate makes tax nil for resident individuals with total income up to ₹12 lakh, but the rebate is not available against tax on special-rate income such as short-term gains on listed equity and long-term gains above the exemption. Check the client's full income before relying on the rebate.
