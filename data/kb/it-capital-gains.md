## Scope and effective date
The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026 and governs income from tax year 2026-27 onwards. The new Act uses the single term "tax year" in place of "previous year" and "assessment year". Many section numbers changed. The rules for capital gains on listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds correspond to sections 111A (short-term) and 112A (long-term) of the 1961 Act. Returns for financial year 2025-26 still follow the 1961 Act.

## Holding period for listed equity and equity-oriented funds
Listed equity shares and units of equity-oriented mutual funds are long-term capital assets when held for more than 12 months. If they are held for 12 months or less, the gain on transfer is short-term. The holding period is counted for each purchase (lot) separately, so units bought through monthly SIP instalments each have their own 12-month clock.

## Short-term capital gains rate
Short-term capital gains on listed equity shares and units of equity-oriented funds, where securities transaction tax applies, are taxed at 20%. This rate applies to transfers on or after 23 July 2024.

## Long-term capital gains and the ₹1.25 lakh exemption
Long-term capital gains on listed equity shares and equity-oriented fund units are taxed at 12.5% on the amount above ₹1.25 lakh in a year, without indexation. The ₹1.25 lakh threshold applies to the total of such long-term gains in the year, not separately to each investment. Booking gains up to the unused part of the threshold within the year (often called tax-gain harvesting) can reduce future tax.

## Grandfathering for purchases before 1 February 2018
For listed equity shares and equity-oriented fund units acquired before 1 February 2018, the cost of acquisition is the higher of the actual cost and the lower of the fair market value on 31 January 2018 and the sale value. This protects gains that accrued before long-term gains on equity became taxable.

## Debt-oriented mutual funds bought on or after 1 April 2023
Gains on units of specified (debt-oriented) mutual funds acquired on or after 1 April 2023 are treated as short-term capital gains and taxed at the investor's slab rate, whatever the holding period. This corresponds to section 50AA of the 1961 Act.

## Setting off capital losses
Short-term capital losses can be set off against both short-term and long-term capital gains. Long-term capital losses can be set off only against long-term capital gains. Losses that cannot be absorbed can be carried forward for up to eight years, provided the return for the year of the loss was filed on time.
